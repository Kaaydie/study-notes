$R_{1}=\{ (a,a),(b,b),(c,c),(d,d),(\underbrace{ a,b }_{  }),(b,c),(a,c),(\underbrace{ b,a }_{  }) \}$
- $R_{1}\text{ is reflective}$

## Antisymetry:
$\forall(x,y)\epsilon R_{1}\land(y,x)\epsilon R_{1}\implies x=y$
We have $(a,b)\epsilon R_{1} \land(b,a)\epsilon R_{1}\text{ , so }R_{1}\text{ is not antisymetric hence not a partial order}$

---

$R_{2}=\{(a,a),(b,b),(c,c),(d,d),(a,b),(b,c),(a,c) \}$
$R_{2}$ is antisymetric no instances of $(x,y)\epsilon R_{2} \land (y,x)\epsilon R_{2}for \ x\neq y$

## Transitivity
$\forall(x,y)\epsilon R_{2}\land(y,z)\epsilon R_{2}\implies(x,z)\epsilon R_{2}$
$(a,b)\epsilon R_{2}\land(b,c)\epsilon R_{2} \land(a,c)\epsilon R_{2}$
$R_{2}$ is transitive hence $R_{2}$ is a partial order on X

## is $R_{2}$ a total order?
-> Is $R_{2}$ connective?
$\forall x,y\epsilon X$ $(x,y)\epsilon R_{2}\lor(y,x)\epsilon R_{2}$
But we have
$b,d\epsilon X$
neither $(b,d)\epsilon R_{2}$ nor $(d,b)\epsilon R_{2}$
Hence $R_{2}$is not connective therefore it is not a total order
